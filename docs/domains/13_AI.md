# AI Domain Specification

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Approved
**Document ID:** DOMAIN-013

---

# 1. Purpose

The AI domain provides intelligent capabilities that enhance the marketplace through analysis, classification, recommendation, automation, and decision support.

The AI domain never owns business entities or business decisions.

It augments existing domains while preserving their autonomy and invariants.

---

# 2. Responsibilities

The AI domain owns:

- AI jobs
- AI workflows
- AI analyses
- AI recommendations
- AI prompts
- AI model orchestration
- AI provider abstraction
- AI confidence scores
- AI execution history

The AI domain does not own:

- Listings
- Orders
- Payments
- Shipping
- Reviews
- Users
- Moderation decisions
- Notifications

Business ownership always remains with the originating domain.

---

# 4. AI Job Responsibilities

An AI Job represents one unit of AI work.

Examples include:

- listing analysis;
- image analysis;
- category prediction;
- size extraction;
- fraud analysis;
- recommendation generation;
- translation;
- moderation assistance.

Jobs are asynchronous.

---

# 5. AI Analysis Responsibilities

AI Analysis represents structured knowledge extracted from business data.

Examples:

- detected brand;
- condition estimate;
- missing attributes;
- duplicate detection;
- quality score;
- fraud indicators;
- semantic embedding.

Analyses are immutable.

New executions create new analyses.

---

# 6. AI Recommendations

Recommendations suggest actions.

Examples:

- improve title;
- improve description;
- adjust price;
- add missing photos;
- merge duplicate listings;
- suggest category.

Recommendations never execute themselves automatically unless explicitly permitted by platform policy.

---

# 7. Invariants

### INV-001

Every AI Job has exactly one objective.

---

### INV-002

Every AI Job produces zero or more AI Analyses.

---

### INV-003

AI never owns business entities.

---

### INV-004

AI recommendations are advisory by default.

---

### INV-005

Business domains remain authoritative.

---

### INV-006

Prompt versions are immutable.

---

### INV-007

Every AI execution records:

- model;
- prompt version;
- timestamp;
- provider;
- execution result.

---

### INV-008

AI confidence scores are immutable once published.

---

### INV-009

AI outputs are versioned.

Historical outputs remain available for audit and comparison.

---

```
Created

↓

Queued

↓

↓

Completed
```

Alternative terminal states:

```
Failed

Canceled

Expired
```

Retries create new executions.

↓

Provider Selection

↓

Validation

---

# 10. Provider Abstraction

Supported providers are interchangeable.

Examples:

- OpenAI
- Anthropic
- Google Gemini
- Mistral
- Azure OpenAI
- Local Models

Provider-specific logic belongs exclusively to infrastructure adapters.

---

# 11. Prompt Management

Prompts are versioned artifacts.

Each prompt records:

- version;
- purpose;
- expected output schema;
- supported models;
- deprecation status.

Prompt changes never overwrite previous versions.

---

# 12. Structured Output

AI responses should be converted into structured domain objects whenever possible.

Examples:

Instead of:

"This looks like Nike sneakers."

Store:

Brand = Nike

Confidence = 0.98

Structured outputs are preferred over free text.

---

# 13. Confidence Scores

Every prediction may include confidence.

Examples:

Brand

98%

Category

95%

Color

99%

Condition

72%

Confidence scores assist decision making.

They never determine business truth.

---

# 14. Human Oversight

AI may:

- recommend;
- classify;
- prioritize;
- summarize.

AI must not independently perform irreversible actions such as:

- deleting Listings;
- banning Users;
- issuing refunds;
- approving moderation decisions;
- modifying financial records.

Such actions always require business-domain authorization or explicit human approval.

---

# 15. Domain Interaction

Business domains request AI capabilities through well-defined interfaces.

Examples:

Listings

→ image analysis

Search

→ semantic indexing

Moderation

→ abuse detection

Messaging

→ translation

Reviews

→ summarization

Administration

→ operational insights

Communication occurs through events, commands, or service interfaces.

# 17. Validation

API

- job request schema;
- supported task type.

Application

- entity existence;
- authorization;
- workflow eligibility.

Domain

- lifecycle transitions;
- prompt compatibility;
- output schema validation.

---

# 18. Error Scenarios

Examples:

- AIJobNotFound
- ProviderUnavailable
- ModelTimeout
- InvalidPromptVersion
- UnsupportedTask
- InvalidStructuredOutput
- ConfidenceUnavailable

Errors must be deterministic and recoverable where possible.

---

# 19. Background Jobs

Background processing includes:

- AI queue execution;
- embedding generation;
- model retries;
- prompt evaluation;
- batch analysis;
- recommendation refresh;
- cache invalidation.

---

# 20. Audit Requirements

The following actions are audited:

- job creation;
- prompt version usage;
- model selection;
- provider selection;
- execution result;
- recommendation publication.

AI history is immutable.

---

# 21. Concurrency Requirements

Duplicate AI Jobs for the same objective may be deduplicated according to platform policy.

Model retries create new executions without overwriting previous results.

Concurrent executions must preserve execution history.

---

# 22. Security Requirements

AI providers receive only the minimum data required to complete a task.

Sensitive information should be anonymized or excluded where possible.

Provider credentials are managed through secure infrastructure.

Prompt injection and malicious model outputs must be validated before consumption.

---

# 23. Compliance Requirements

AI processing must comply with applicable privacy, consumer protection, intellectual property, and AI regulations.

Users should be informed when legally required that AI-assisted functionality is being used.

AI-generated outputs must remain traceable to their originating execution.

---

# 24. Non-Goals

The AI domain does not implement:

- marketplace business rules;
- authentication;
- authorization;
- payment processing;
- shipment management;
- moderation decisions;
- business data ownership.

---

# 25. Extension Points

Future enhancements include:

- multi-agent orchestration;
- autonomous workflow planning;
- retrieval-augmented generation (RAG);
- knowledge graph integration;
- reinforcement learning from platform feedback;
- multimodal reasoning;
- personalized assistants;
- agent-to-agent collaboration;
- continuous prompt evaluation;
- model A/B testing.

Extensions must preserve domain ownership, auditability, and deterministic business behavior.

---

# 26. Acceptance Criteria

The AI domain is complete when:

- AI functionality is isolated from business ownership;
- provider implementations are interchangeable;
- prompts are versioned;
- AI outputs are structured and versioned;
- confidence scores are preserved;
- recommendations are advisory by default;
- all executions are auditable;
- domain events are emitted correctly;
- automated tests validate provider abstraction, workflow execution, structured output validation, retry behavior, and security controls.
